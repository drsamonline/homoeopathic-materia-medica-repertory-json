## Why there is no OCR damage here

This is not a scan. The text came from Gutenberg as a typed page, so every word is
correct as printed — a rarity in this library, where most volumes carry the scan's
damage ("ABJCES" for ABIES).

## How the book divides

Hill writes by **disease**, not by remedy: a disease name stands alone on its line,
a description follows, and then a `TREATMENT.` block names the remedies with the
dilutions he advises. The parse takes each disease name as a section and keeps the
`TREATMENT.` block inside it, verbatim, because that is the book's own shape.

The appendix sections — `LOCAL APPLICATIONS`, `TO PREVENT CHOLERA`, `TO PREVENT
ITCH`, `PREPARATION OF MEDICINE` — are printed as headings in the book and are kept
as sections.

## Known limitations

- **Two non-sections were removed** rather than published as chapters: the printer's
  district line from the title page, and a sentence fragment the heading rule caught
  inside Small Pox.
- **No grades.** Hill prints dilutions (1, 2, 3), not repertory grades, so no remedy
  links are extracted; the remedies stay in the prose exactly as he set them.
- **One source, no corroboration** — though a typed transcription needs none in the
  way a scan does.
